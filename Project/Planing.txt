Step 1. Define the question. State what is being optimized, predicted, controlled, learned, or
compared. Avoid starting from an algorithm without first identifying the problem.

Step 2. Formulate the model. Identify variables, parameters, objective functions, constraints,
states/actions, losses, probability distributions, or game-theoretic players as appropriate.
1
Step 3. Choose a baseline. Begin with the simplest correct method. Examples include linear re-
gression before a neural network, centralized training before federated learning, value iteration
before Q-learning, or a weighted sum before NSGA-II.
Step 4. Implement and validate. Test the method first on a small example where the expected
behavior is known. When possible, compare numerical output with a closed-form solution.
Step 5. Design experiments. Decide in advance which questions the experiments answer. Vary
meaningful parameters and report appropriate metrics.
Step 6. Analyze, do not only report. Explain why methods behave differently, what assumptions
matter, and where the model fails.
Step 7. Extend the baseline. Add at least one substantive extension: robustness, regularization,
alternative objectives, heterogeneous data, uncertainty, another algorithm, sensitivity analy-
sis, or a more realistic dataset.
Step 8. Prepare the report and presentation. Present the mathematical formulation before
computational results and connect conclusions explicitly to the course concepts.
